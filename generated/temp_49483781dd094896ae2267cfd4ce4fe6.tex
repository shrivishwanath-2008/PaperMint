\section*{1. Average Velocity and Average Speed}
Average Velocity is defined as the change in position or displacement ($\Delta x$) divided by the time intervals ($\Delta t$):
\[ \bar{v} = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \]
Average Speed is defined as the total path length travelled divided by the total time interval:
\[ \text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Path Length}}{\text{Total Time Interval}} \]

\section*{2. Instantaneous Velocity and Acceleration}
Instantaneous Velocity ($v$) is the velocity at a specific instant of time:
\[ v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{dx}{dt} \]
Instantaneous Acceleration ($a$) is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time:
\[ a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} \]
Acceleration can also be expressed as:
\[ a = v \frac{dv}{dx} \]

\section*{3. Kinematic Equations for Uniformly Accelerated Motion}
For an object moving with constant acceleration $a$, initial velocity $u$, final velocity $v$, and displacement $s$ in time $t$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $v = u + at$
    \item $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
    \item $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
    \item $s = \left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)t$
    \item Displacement in the $n^{th}$ second: $s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$
\end{itemize}

\section*{4. Motion Under Gravity (Free Fall)}
Taking the upward direction as positive, the acceleration due to gravity is $a = -g$ (where $g \approx 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item $v = u - gt$
    \item $h = ut - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
    \item $v^2 = u^2 - 2gh$
\end{itemize}
For an object dropped from rest ($u = 0$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Time to reach the ground: $t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}$
    \item Velocity on reaching the ground: $v = \sqrt{2gh}$
\end{itemize}

\section*{5. Relative Velocity}
Relative velocity of object $A$ with respect to object $B$:
\[ v_{AB} = v_A - v_B \]
Relative velocity of object $B$ with respect to object $A$:
\[ v_{BA} = v_B - v_A \]

\section*{6. Important Graphical Relations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The slope of the Position-Time ($x-t$) graph represents the velocity.
    \item The slope of the Velocity-Time ($v-t$) graph represents the acceleration.
    \item The area under the Velocity-Time ($v-t$) graph represents the displacement.
    \item The area under the Acceleration-Time ($a-t$) graph represents the change in velocity.
\end{itemize}

\section*{7. Stopping Distance and Reaction Time}
Stopping distance ($d_s$) of a vehicle:
\[ d_s = \frac{-u^2}{2a} \]
Reaction time ($t_r$) calculated from a ruler drop test:
\[ t_r = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} \]
